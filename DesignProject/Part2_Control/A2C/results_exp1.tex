\subsection{Results}
\label{sec:exp1}

We trained PPO and A2C across all combinations of three design choices: the
reward function (the \emph{base} task reward vs.\ the \emph{tuned} shaped
reward), the observation encoding (raw $[\theta,\omega]$ vs.\ the
wrap-invariant $[\sin\theta,\cos\theta,\omega]$), and the disturbance setting
(nominal vs.\ \emph{robust} training with actuator noise and dropped commands).
Each configuration was run with \textbf{six random seeds} (42--47); every run
was capped at $10^6$ steps with early stopping, which also halted training as
soon as the evaluation return reached $180$ (i.e.\ $0.9\times$ the maximum
attainable return of $200$). We report, aggregated as mean~$\pm$~std over seeds,
the environment steps trained, the wall-clock training time, and the mean return
over 10 evaluation episodes (Table~\ref{tab:exp1}); the design is paired
(matched seeds), so the comparisons in Table~\ref{tab:exp1_tests} are run within
seed blocks using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test with bootstrap confidence
intervals.

\paragraph{PPO decisively outperforms A2C.}
PPO reaches a mean return of $172.6$ (median $180.5$) against A2C's $66.0$
(median $92.2$), while training about four times faster ($3.0$ vs.\ $12.6$ min).
The separation is clean: A2C's \emph{best} single run ($137.1$) still falls
below PPO's \emph{worst} ($145.5$), and $15$ of the $36$ A2C runs effectively
fail to learn the swing-up (return $<30$). The advantage is large and
significant in the paired test (\mbox{$\Delta=+106.6$}, 95\%~CI
$[+93,+119]$, $d_z=2.6$, Wilcoxon $p<10^{-10}$; Table~\ref{tab:exp1_tests}), and
since the PPO side is censored at the $180$ cap this gap is in fact a
\emph{lower bound}. A2C is also far less reproducible: a Levene test rejects
equal variance ($p=1.7\times10^{-5}$; std.\ $36.1$ vs.\ $12.3$), with per-config
seed spread reaching a coefficient of variation of ${\sim}78\%$ for A2C against
${\le}4\%$ for PPO. On this task A2C is therefore not competitive.

\paragraph{A censored ceiling near $180$.}
The strong PPO configurations converge to almost exactly the early-stopping cap
($\approx 181$): $22$ of the $72$ runs --- \emph{all} of them PPO\,$\cdot$\,sin/cos
--- hit $180$ and were stopped, so their reported return is right-censored. Final
return therefore cannot rank these configurations against each other (they are
equal by construction), and the only fair discriminator is how \emph{quickly} and
how \emph{reliably} each reaches the cap. On steps-to-threshold the best
controller is \texttt{PPO, tuned reward, sin/cos, nominal}, which reaches $180$ in
$167$k steps ($1.4$ min) on all $6$ seeds.

\paragraph{The sin/cos encoding is the one decisive design factor.}
For PPO, the wrap-invariant encoding yields a real and significant return gain
over the raw angle (\mbox{$\Delta=+23.9$}, 95\%~CI $[+19,+29]$, $d_z=2.7$,
$p<10^{-3}$); because the sin/cos side is censored, the true gain is again at
least this large. Reward shaping is at most second-order for PPO
(\mbox{$\Delta=+5.0$}, $p=0.027$) and irrelevant for A2C. For A2C the sin/cos
encoding actually \emph{hurts} ($\Delta=-32.9$, $p=0.032$), consistent with the
weaker learner struggling with the harder-to-exploit representation.

\paragraph{Robust training is effective but not free.}
Robust-trained PPO still reaches the cap, so it costs no \emph{final return}
(\mbox{$\Delta=-1.3$}, CI $[-3.4,+0.2]$, $p=1.0$) --- but this apparent
``no-cost'' result is an artifact of censoring. On the uncensored metric the
trade is visible: robust training needs more steps to reach $180$ (e.g.\
$167$k\,$\to$\,$342$k for the tuned reward) and, in that setting, only $4/6$
seeds reach the cap at all (vs.\ $6/6$ nominal). Robust training therefore buys
disturbance tolerance for sim-to-real at a sample-efficiency and reliability
cost, rather than for free.

\paragraph{More compute did not help.}
Return correlates negatively with training time ($r=-0.81$ across all runs,
$r=-0.62$ within PPO): the longest runs are the failed A2C runs that trained for
many minutes yet scored low, while the best PPO controllers hit the cap and
stopped within a couple of minutes. Performance is thus set by the algorithm and
the observation encoding rather than by the compute budget, and early stopping
correctly halted converged runs well before the $10^6$-step limit. Reporting
mean~$\pm$~std over six seeds removes the single-seed confound of the earlier
write-up, turning the previously ``confounded'' secondary effects into the
quantified, significance-tested results above.
